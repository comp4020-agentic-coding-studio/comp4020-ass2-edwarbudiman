## The disagreement

**Named disagreement.** `designer` frames the round as a synthesis with no real split. That framing does not survive: it papers over a genuine three-way disagreement about *what is missing* and, downstream of that, what fixing it costs.

`committee`'s diagnosis: the ladder is missing a **measurement** — week 3 stages a calibration finding (self-rate, explain, re-rate) and `LAD-1`'s seven rungs are all performance constructs, so the gap that opens in week 3 is never checked against anything. Its remedy is deliberately minimal and declarative: one paragraph on `IA-4` stating the ladder measures performance, not calibration — no new mechanism, no eighth rung, nothing for standing test 4 to fail.

`student` denies this describes her case, and `designer` adopts her denial rather than treating it as a side note: *"You can't score a judgement somebody never made."* Her diagnosis relocates the fault one step earlier — not a missing measurement of a self-estimate, but a missing **occasion** on which any prior estimate is ever produced. Free retrieval didn't make her worse at judging her own understanding; it removed the moment where a judgement used to get forced out of her as a by-product of having to draft blind. `designer` takes this diagnosis as correct and states plainly that `committee`'s fix, built on the wrong diagnosis, "declares the right kind of omission at the wrong depth."

The real split, then, is not about whether something is missing at week 3 — all three agree something is — but about **what kind of fix a correct diagnosis licenses**. `committee` holds that even under its own (contested) diagnosis, the fix should stay declarative: name the gap on `IA-4`, add no check, because the alternative (an eighth rung, or anything crossing rungs by conditions) was already shown in Round 1 to break on arithmetic. `designer`, having accepted `student`'s sharper diagnosis, holds that naming is not enough once the missing thing is correctly identified as an absent *elicitation*: Proposal 1 requires the examiner to actually take a sealed prior-state specimen at week 3, checked via a narrowed `HAR-11` entry on every assessment. `committee` has not yet been asked to react to `student`'s diagnosis or to Proposal 1 built on it — its recorded position addresses only the ladder's missing construct, stated before `student` or `designer` spoke.

What turns on it: whether `IA-4` gains a single uncheckable sentence (`committee`'s standing fix) or a required elicitation step reaching into week 3, `HAR-11` (every assessment's page), and — as a conceded, priced side effect — `A1`, a `DECIDED` assessment that fails the new check on its face and would read as deficient on its own page for the first time in the record. It also bears on whether `committee`'s refusal of "any scale of change beyond declaration" is a settled position or one it hasn't yet weighed against the diagnosis it would have to accept to be pressed on it.
