## The disagreement

**Named disagreement.** `committee` holds that the submission's niche-ness currently rests entirely on two `PROPOSED` items (`VOI-3`, `CON-7`) and that the `DECIDED` core beneath them is a course it "would approve in ninety seconds and forget." `designer` and `student` (Tess) both deny the inference that follows from that — not the fact that `VOI-3`/`CON-7` are unruled, which all three accept, but the reading that unruled therefore means peripheral, ornamental, or safely deferrable.

The real split is over what `VOI-3` *is* to this submission. For `committee` it is one candidate condition among several still open, weighed the same way any `PROPOSED` item is weighed pending a ruling. For `student`, reading cold, it is the one sentence in the whole brief that gets her to enrol at all: *"Strip it and I don't enrol — not because the course got broader, but because it went quiet about the thing I came for."* For `designer`, it is not decoration bolted onto an already-complete course but the substantive answer to this round's own question — his two proposals (the ladder's second axis, A2's countersignature) are attempts to *harden* `VOI-3`/`CON-7` into checks the harness enforces, not to argue for keeping them as-is.

What turns on it: whether the chair's eventual ruling on `OQ-1` (niche-ness) can be written against the `DECIDED` set alone, treating `VOI-3` and `CON-7` as later, optional refinement — or whether it must rule `VOI-3` and `CON-7` in as load-bearing before niche-ness can be said to hold at all, which is what commits the harness to `HAR-13`/`HAR-14` work `designer` has already priced. It also bears on ID-4: `designer` conceded the catalogue line can no longer promise improved understanding: whether it can promise anything about `VOI-3` depends on which reading of this disagreement stands.
