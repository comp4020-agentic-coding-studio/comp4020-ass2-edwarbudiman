| Week    | Lecture focus                         | Core question                                                                        | Main content to cover                                                                                                                                                                                         | In-class / experiential anchor                                                                                                                                | Failure that must be produced                                                                                                               | What it hands to next week                                                                              |
| ------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **W1**  | **When information is cheap**         | If answers are everywhere, what does it mean to know something?                      | Cheap retrieval; familiarity vs possession of knowledge; information availability; why access is not itself evidence of understanding; strongest argument *against* needing internal knowledge                | Give students a concept/problem with unrestricted access to search/AI, then ask what exactly their successful answer demonstrates                             | Access and successful retrieval can look like knowledge without telling us what the person can independently produce                        | We need some way to expose what is inside the learner rather than what the environment can supply       |
| **W2**  | **The feeling of understanding**      | How do we discover that something we thought we understood is missing?               | Familiarity, recognition, illusion of explanatory depth; confidence vs performance; explaining as a probe rather than proof                                                                                   | First version of the **knowledge paper-swap** activity: one student supplies a known topic, another researches it freely, explains it, original knower probes | A fluent explanation can still conceal missing relations, causes, boundary conditions, or dependencies                                      | If explanation is insufficient, perhaps reconstruction with the source removed gives better evidence   |
| **W3**  | **Reconstruction**                    | What becomes visible when the answer is no longer in front of us?                    | Consumption vs reconstruction; recall/reconstruction distinction; rebuilding structure rather than repeating text; gaps revealed by source removal                                                            | Study something, remove access, reconstruct it, then restore the source and annotate what was lost/changed                                                    | Reconstruction is stronger evidence, but can still be memorised, rehearsed, or assisted                                                     | We need to compare performances rather than treat one task as decisive                                  |
| **W4**  | **Evidence changes with conditions**  | Why does the same person look more or less knowledgeable under different conditions? | Comparing evidence; task conditions; the danger of treating performance as transparent; introduce idea that evidence is produced by a situation                                                               | Give one concept under two or three different conditions and compare judgments                                                                                | Intuitive judgements become inconsistent across tasks and conditions                                                                        | We now need an explicit instrument rather than intuition                                                |
| **W5**  | **Build the first instrument**        | Can we systematically distinguish stronger and weaker evidence of understanding?     | Introduce/build the ladder; operational definitions; recognition/recall/explanation/application/connection/etc. as candidate performances; why the ladder is an **instrument**, not a theory of understanding | Students classify earlier course examples using the ladder and disagree about difficult cases                                                                 | The ladder makes judgement systematic, but silently assumes certain performances are intrinsically better evidence                          | Now attack those assumptions one at a time                                                              |
| **W6**  | **What are you allowed to use?**      | Does needing support show that you do not understand?                                | Source present vs absent; books, notes, documentation, calculators, reference material; distributed/external support; memory vs competence                                                                    | Same task under different support conditions                                                                                                                  | Removing support can reveal dependence, but can also accidentally test memory rather than understanding                                     | Evidence depends not just on the task, but on what resources belong legitimately inside the performance |
| **W7**  | **Change the form**                   | If someone can use the idea differently, is that better evidence?                    | Representation; audience; application; analogy; explanation across contexts; transfer; adaptation                                                                                                             | Same concept, unexpected output mode/audience/problem                                                                                                         | Reformatting or adaptation can also be rehearsed; apparent transfer may rely on prior templates/domain familiarity                          | Preparation itself affects how informative a performance is                                             |
| **W8**  | **What if you didn't know the test?** | What does surprise reveal that preparation can hide?                                 | Notice; rehearsed vs unannounced performance; follow-up probes; adversarial/diagnostic questioning; why surprise can reveal model structure                                                                   | Prepared question followed by an unforeseen “why / what if / what would change your answer?”                                                                  | Surprise produces useful evidence, but final performance still hides what existed before the probe                                          | We need evidence of the learner's state before new information/intervention                             |
| **W9**  | **The prior state**                   | What can we learn from what someone thought before checking?                         | Pre-commitment; predictions; before/after states; revision; calibration only after a prior claim exists; wrong-first-answers as evidence                                                                      | Student commits to prediction/explanation before sources, then researches, revises and explains why                                                           | A final correct answer erases the path by which it was reached; prior states help, but still do not tell us how much assistance contributed | The next problem is collaboration: whose cognition/performance are we looking at?                       |
| **W10** | **Knowledge with a collaborator**     | What are we measuring when AI or another support system participates?                | AI as collaborator rather than cheating object; attribution of performance; tool use; legitimate support; person vs person-plus-environment; assisted expertise                                               | Repeat knowledge-swap exercise, but learner may actively use AI during questioning/problem solving                                                            | The clean boundary between “their knowledge” and “the tool's contribution” becomes difficult to defend                                      | The examiner must still make a practical judgement despite all these complications                      |
| **W11** | **Administering an examination**      | How do you actually find out what someone understands?                               | Instrument design; task selection; resources; notice; prior states; follow-ups; administration; fairness; what an instrument cannot detect; difference between knowing a topic and being able to examine it   | Students design and administer a real examination to another person                                                                                           | A pass/fail/performance still does not interpret itself; the examiner must make an inference from incomplete evidence                       | Final problem: what are we justified in concluding?                                                     |
| **W12** | **Defending the inference**           | What can we responsibly claim from the evidence we collected?                        | Validity as inference; uncertainty; false positives/negatives; instrument limitations; AI-assisted subject who passes every rung; useful vs sufficient evidence; critique of the ladder                       | Hard case where the subject passes or fails in ways that resist a clean conclusion                                                                            | Even strong instruments cannot turn observed performance into certainty about understanding                                                 | Course ends with a disciplined limitation of claims rather than a perfect test                          |
