# Build the first instrument

Can we systematically distinguish stronger and weaker evidence of
understanding?

Week 5 · A self-guided lesson

---

## Two choices in building this ladder

Week 2 used recognition to mean a feeling of familiarity. Rung 1 now requires an observable recognition response; that is limited evidence, not the feeling alone. Application and connection were candidate performances in our plan. Here we judge them through the defined rungs—for example, explaining a new case or justifying a connection—rather than making them separate rungs.

---

## Retrieve your Week 4 rule · 2 minutes

Bring back the sentence “I counted that as stronger evidence because…” exactly as you wrote it. After learning the seven rungs, identify the rung your rule rewards and one performance it cannot distinguish. Preserve the original, then write a revised rule. If you have no record, use this provisional rule: “I counted a correct answer without the reference as stronger evidence.”

---

## Worked comparison

The fallback rule rewards source removal but cannot distinguish Recall from Reconstruction: both can happen without a reference. A revised rule could require the relations between parts to be rebuilt, not just the parts named. Resource conditions still belong in the record. A different original rule may map to a different rung; explain the match.

---

## How to use this lesson

Read at your own pace; no presenter is needed. Allow around 40 minutes. Have
somewhere to write a few short answers.

At a **Pause** slide, attempt the task before advancing. The next slide or two
explain the reasoning so you can compare it with yours.

You need nothing from Weeks 1–4 open in front of you — every performance this
lesson uses is quoted again here. Sam, Theo, Priya and Rowan are the
fictional people those weeks already introduced; nothing new is invented about
any of them today.

---

## Where Week 4 left us

Week 4 gave one person, Rowan, three performances on one lock under three
conditions. Two careful readers could reach opposite verdicts from the same
texts, with no way to show each other where they went wrong.

It ended by naming what was missing: not more evidence, but a rule for
**weighing** the evidence already in hand — written down before you look, so it
can be inspected and argued with.

---

## A loose preview, now made precise

Week 4 previewed this loosely, as a ladder running "from recognising something
up to connecting it to other things." That phrase was a placeholder.

Naming the actual top rung was this week's job, not last week's. Here is the
precise version.

---

## What an instrument is

An **instrument** is a stated rule for turning a performance into a judgement,
written *before* you look at the case it will be applied to.

It is not a theory of what understanding **is**. It makes no claim about what
happens inside a learner's head.

It is a tool for making your own judgement inspectable — so a disagreement can
be about which rung applies, not about who sounded more convincing.

---

## You have already used two

Week 1's evidence record — Observation, Interpretation, Limit — was a small
instrument.

Week 3's four-letter scheme — Part, Relation, Causal step, Condition omitted —
was a second one, built for one kind of performance.

This week's ladder is broader: one scale for almost any performance the course
produces from here on.

---

{/* _class: impact */}

## Each rung is defined by what a performance does

Not by how confident, fluent or long it is.

A short, hesitant performance can reach a high rung. A long, fluent one can
fail to reach a low one. This lesson will show you both.

---

## Rung 1 — Recognition

**"I've seen this."**

The person correctly matches or repeats something already stated, with it
available to consult or freshly in view.

The weakest rung: it requires identifying, not producing.

---

## Rung 2 — Recall

**"I can retrieve this without the source in front of me."**

The person produces a specific piece of material — a term, a fact, a step —
from memory, with nothing to consult.

Recall returns material. It does not yet require that material to be correctly
related to anything else.

---

## Rung 3 — Reconstruction

**"I can rebuild the relationships between the parts."**

Week 3's definition, unchanged: naming the parts, placing them relative to one
another, giving the causal steps — without the source in view.

A list of correct terms with nothing joining them is Recall, not
Reconstruction.

---

## Rung 4 — Explanation

**"I can make this intelligible to someone else."**

The person produces the structure for an audience: recast, ordered, pitched so
that *another person* — not just the explainer — could follow it.

A different demand from Reconstruction, which only has to satisfy the person
producing it.

---

## Rung 5 — Justification

**"I can support the claims inside my explanation."**

Not only asserting a causal step, but giving the reason it holds — the
"because" a listener could doubt and ask about.

An explanation can be complete and still stop short of this.

---

## Rung 6 — Challenge

**"I can respond when someone attacks my assumptions, evidence or
relationships."**

Someone else raises a specific objection, and the person answers it — by
defending, revising, or showing why the objection misses.

This rung cannot be produced by a person working alone.

---

## Rung 7 — Boundary

**"I know where my explanation stops working."**

The person names, unprompted, a condition under which their own account would
fail, become uncertain, or need to be checked.

The edge of their own model, found by looking — not told by someone else.

<p className="source-note">The seven rungs are this course's own construction, built for this course's question — not drawn from Bloom's taxonomy or any other published scale.</p>

---

## Two things the ladder is not

**Not strictly ordered.** A performance can show Boundary-level awareness
without ever cleanly showing Justification.

**Not a checklist.** A short technical question may have no occasion for a
Challenge or a Boundary at all — that is not a failure of the person
answering.

Treat the rungs as seven *kinds* of evidence, not seven steps everyone must
climb in order.

---

## It is also not settled

Later weeks attack assumptions built into this ladder. This week names one of
them. Week 12 attacks the ladder itself, once you have used it for a term.

A course that presented its own instrument as finished would be doing the
thing it exists to criticise.

## Pause and classify · 8 minutes

Five performances follow, all from earlier weeks, quoted exactly as they
appeared. For each: which rung does it reach, and what is the obvious
objection to your answer?

Write one line per performance before advancing. Attempt all five.

---

## A. Sam — Week 1

Reading the reference with it open:

> "Wi-Fi and internet access are different."

---

## B. Theo — Week 2

Writing from memory, nothing in front of him:

> "The slider pulls the two sides of teeth together and they interlock like
> fingers. Pulling it back down separates them again."

---

## C. Priya — Week 3

Reconstructing the lock from memory:

> "A door lock has a barrel that turns when you put the right key in. Inside
> there are pins of different heights. The key pushes the pins up to the right
> heights, and then the barrel can turn and the door opens. A wrong key does
> not push them to the right heights, so it will not turn."

---

## D. Rowan — Week 4, condition (b)

Explaining to a novice, reference closed:

> "Every pin is a different length and every notch is a different depth. The
> right key lifts each pin by exactly its own amount, so all of them come
> flush with that join at the same moment."

---

## E. Rowan — Week 4, condition (c)

Predicting an unfamiliar case:

> "I think it does not matter that the other five are fine. One is enough.
> Because the barrel is one piece... Would you even be able to tell this key
> from a totally wrong one, from the outside? I do not think you would. That
> feels wrong to me but I cannot see what would make it false."

---

## Worked response: A — Sam

A strict reading holds him at **Recognition**: the source was open, and
repeating a stated sentence with it in view does not yet require retrieving
anything unaided.

A looser reading calls it **Recall** — Sam produced the sentence rather than
pointing at it.

The disagreement turns entirely on whether "the source was available" changes
*which rung applies*, or only how much we trust reaching it. Hold that
question; a later slide comes back to it.

---

## Worked response: B — Theo

The task he attempted was **Explanation** — nothing in front of him, for a
lesson that assumed a naive reader.

Whether his *performance* reaches it is separate. His sentence names an
outcome ("they interlock like fingers") without the mechanism that produces
it, and omits what holds one tooth to the next.

A generous reading credits **Recall** — real, correct material, retrieved. The
missing causal step is exactly what Explanation requires, and his sentence
does not supply it.

---

## Worked response: C — Priya

She attempted **Reconstruction** — nothing in front of her, asked to rebuild
relationships, not list terms.

Her performance is thin at exactly the place Reconstruction is defined: four
parts missing, and the one relation she states ("right heights, so it turns")
is a rule with no mechanism behind it.

Scored by task attempted: Reconstruction. Scored by what actually got
reconstructed: closer to Recall. Both readings are defensible.

---

## Worked response: D — Rowan (b)

**Explanation** by task — source-free, pitched for a novice — and it is
fluent, complete in outline, and wrong in one place: the two-pin stack has
collapsed into a single pin.

This shows more than Theo's — structure, an audience, independent phrasing —
and it still misdescribes the mechanism.

**Reaching a rung is not the same as reaching it accurately.** The ladder
tells you what kind of evidence you have, not whether that evidence is good
news.

---

## Worked response: E — Rowan (c), part one

*"...because the barrel is one piece"* — a reason for a claim. This is
**Justification**: it says why, not just that.

*"...I cannot see what would make it false"* — Rowan naming, unprompted, the
edge of what this performance can show from the outside. This is
**Boundary**.

---

## Worked response: E — Rowan (c), part two

Notice what is missing in between: nothing here is **Challenge**. Nobody
attacked Rowan's account and made them defend it — the hedged opening ("do you
want me to guess?") is uncertainty about the genre of the task, not a
challenge.

One performance touches rungs 5 and 7 without passing through 6. "Not
strictly ordered," demonstrated rather than asserted.

## The rung nobody produced

Read back across all five. None of Sam, Theo, Priya or Rowan — across all three
conditions — ever answers an objection someone else raised.

Across four weeks of this course, nobody has yet been shown a counterargument
to their own explanation and asked to respond.

---

## Why that gap matters

Challenge cannot be produced by a person working alone, however hard they think
about their own account. It needs a second party who has not already agreed
with them.

An instrument that only measures what a solo performance can supply is missing
a whole rung. The course will need an activity that specifically manufactures
one.

## Pause and write: produce a Challenge · 6 minutes

Take an explanation you wrote in the Week 2 or Week 3 investigation — your own
zip or lock account. If you did not keep one, use Priya's reconstruction.

**The attack:** "Your account never says what happens if two parts are both
slightly wrong at once — does the mechanism fail twice as badly, or is one
wrong part already enough that a second one changes nothing?"

Write your response in three or four sentences. Attempt it before advancing.

---

## Worked response: meeting the Challenge

Using the lock: a correctly working key already has to get every stack level
with the shear line, and a wrong key fails as soon as *one* stack is off. Two
wrong stacks do not make the lock "twice as locked" — it was already fully
stopped by the first one.

The attack is answered by a fact Week 4 already established: the plug is one
object, not five independent ones, so failures do not add up, they duplicate a
verdict the first failure had already reached.

---

## What made that a real response

Restating your original explanation more firmly is **not** a response to a
Challenge. This one does real work against the objection.

Notice it reuses Rowan's condition (c) reasoning: once you have the "one
object, not five" relation, several objections answer themselves the same way.
A good Justification is often the raw material several Challenges are answered
with.

## An assumption hiding in plain sight

Look again at rungs 2 and 3.

Recall: produced *"without the source in front of me."* Reconstruction:
rebuilt *"without the source in view."*

The absence of a resource is not incidental to these definitions. It is
written into them.

---

## Naming the assumption

A performance produced with a reference open cannot, by this wording, count as
Recall or Reconstruction at all — however accurate it is.

**The assumption:** a performance produced without support is intrinsically
stronger evidence than the same performance produced with support.

This week is not the place to defend or attack it. Only to name it.

---

## Where you already met it

Section on Sam already turned on exactly this question: does the source's
presence change *which rung applies*, or only how confidently we trust
reaching it?

Next week attacks the assumption directly: does needing support show that you
do not understand — or only that you were allowed to use what was available?

---

## Keep a short record

Write four lines and keep them:

1. **Rung reached** — the highest rung a performance of yours reaches, honestly assessed.
2. **Contested case** — which of the five you placed differently, and why.
3. **The missing rung** — one sentence on why Challenge cannot be produced alone.
4. **The hidden assumption** — restate it in your own words.

If you agreed with every worked placement, go back and argue the other side of
one before moving on.

complete the lesson.

## What to take from Week 5

The ladder makes disagreement inspectable. You can now say exactly which rung
you placed a performance at, and someone else can argue you out of it — which
Week 4 could not do.

It has not made judgement automatic. It has a specific, named assumption
sitting inside two of its own rungs.

Next week holds a mechanism fixed and changes only what a person is allowed to
consult — testing whether needing support is evidence against understanding,
or evidence of nothing at all.
