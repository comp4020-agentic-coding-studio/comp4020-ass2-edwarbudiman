## The brief

> Take something you've already convinced an instrument you understand, and
> use it somewhere that instrument never looked.

Passing a test inside the conditions it was designed for is the weakest
possible evidence of understanding — it's exactly what a memorised answer
would also produce. This assessment asks you to carry a model or skill from
A1 or A2 into a genuinely new case and show it still does work there. Then,
looking back, say what your earlier results actually established and what
they didn't: an instrument that never left its original domain can't have
ruled out rehearsal, however convincing it looked at the time.

This is the course's final and most direct answer to Week 12's unresolved
case — an argument memorised well enough to survive every rung. Transfer to
an unrehearsed domain is the one thing memorisation alone struggles to fake.

## What you submit

An application of your model to the new case, written so a reader with no
access to your earlier submissions can follow and evaluate it, plus a short
retrospective naming exactly what A1 and A2 could and couldn't have shown
about whether you understood the material, versus could reproduce it.
