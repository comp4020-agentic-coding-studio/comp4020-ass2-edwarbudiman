## The brief

> Pick something you don't yet understand, learn it with the source open,
> then find out what you actually kept once it closed.

This is a self-measurement task, not a research report. Choose material
narrow enough to reconstruct in full inside the time you have — a mechanism,
a procedure, an argument — and unfamiliar enough that you can't already
recite it. Read or watch with the source open, close it, and write your
explanation from memory alone. Then reopen the source and mark your own
reconstruction against it, in public, in the submission itself.

The mark isn't for getting it right the first time. It's for locating,
precisely, where the reconstruction diverged from the source, and for the
corrected explanation you produce once you've seen the gap.

## What you submit

A single document with three parts: the reconstruction (written with the
source closed, timestamped or otherwise evidenced as such), the comparison
against the reopened source, and the corrected explanation. Note what your
instrument — this exercise — could and couldn't tell you about whether you
understood the material, as distinct from whether you remembered it.
