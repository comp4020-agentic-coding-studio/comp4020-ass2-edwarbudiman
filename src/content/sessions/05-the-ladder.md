## Two choices in building this ladder

Week 2 used recognition to mean a feeling of familiarity. Rung 1 now requires an observable recognition response; that is limited evidence, not the feeling alone. Application and connection were candidate performances in our plan. Here we judge them through the defined rungs—for example, explaining a new case or justifying a connection—rather than making them separate rungs.

## Retrieve your Week 4 rule · 2 minutes

Bring back the sentence “I counted that as stronger evidence because…” exactly as you wrote it. After learning the seven rungs, identify the rung your rule rewards and one performance it cannot distinguish. Preserve the original, then write a revised rule. If you have no record, use this provisional rule: “I counted a correct answer without the reference as stronger evidence.”

<details>
<summary>Worked comparison</summary>

The fallback rule rewards source removal but cannot distinguish Recall from Reconstruction: both can happen without a reference. A revised rule could require the relations between parts to be rebuilt, not just the parts named. Resource conditions still belong in the record. A different original rule may map to a different rung; explain the match.

</details>

## Purpose and preparation

This self-guided activity applies [Week 5's lesson](/lectures/week-05/). Allow
25 minutes. All required material is quoted below, so you do not need Weeks 1–4
open, though it helps if you have read them. Work alone or with a partner; no
lock, zip, paid tool or AI account is required. Sam, Theo, Priya and
Rowan remain fictional teaching examples, not transcripts.

If you completed the deck's classification exercise, reuse those answers here
rather than starting over — this activity asks you to defend and extend them,
not repeat them.

## 1. Classify five performances — 8 minutes

For each performance below, write the rung it reaches and one sentence
defending that choice against the most obvious objection to it. Do this before
reading the deck's or lecture's worked responses, even if you have already read
them once — writing your own placement is the record this activity keeps.

**A.** Sam (Week 1), reference open: "Wi-Fi and internet access are different."

**B.** Theo (Week 2), from memory: "The slider pulls the two sides of teeth
together and they interlock like fingers. Pulling it back down separates them
again."

**C.** Priya (Week 3), from memory: "A door lock has a barrel that turns when
you put the right key in. Inside there are pins of different heights. The key
pushes the pins up to the right heights, and then the barrel can turn and the
door opens. A wrong key does not push them to the right heights, so it will not
turn."

**D.** Rowan (Week 4, condition b), to a novice, reference closed: "Every pin is
a different length and every notch is a different depth. The right key lifts
each pin by exactly its own amount, so all of them come flush with that join at
the same moment."

**E.** Rowan (Week 4, condition c), predicting an unfamiliar case: "I think it
does not matter that the other five are fine. One is enough. Because the barrel
is one piece... Would you even be able to tell this key from a totally wrong
one, from the outside? I do not think you would."

<details>
<summary>Compare after classifying all five</summary>

A: Recognition on a strict reading (source was open), Recall on a looser one
(Sam produced rather than pointed). B: attempted Explanation, but the missing
causal step ("what holds one tooth to the next") means a strict reading credits
only Recall. C: attempted Reconstruction; scored by task, Reconstruction, but
the missing mechanism behind "right heights, so it turns" pulls a strict
reading back toward Recall. D: reaches Explanation — source-free, pitched for a
novice — but misdescribes the mechanism, so reaching the rung and being correct
are separate findings. E: touches Justification ("because the barrel is one
piece") and Boundary ("I cannot see what would make it false") without ever
passing through Challenge, which nothing here produces at all.

If your placements differ, that is not necessarily an error — the [lecture
notes](/lectures/week-05/) work through exactly why B, C and E are genuinely
contested.

</details>

## 2. Produce the missing rung — 8 minutes

No performance in section 1 reaches Challenge: nobody attacked one of these five
accounts and got a response. That rung cannot be produced by working alone on
your own explanation, however carefully — it needs a second party who has not
already agreed with you.

Take one of your own saved answers from the Week 2 or Week 3 investigation —
your zip, lock, or solo-route mechanism. If you have none saved, use Priya's
reconstruction in section 1.

Here is the attack: **"Your account never says what happens if two parts are
both slightly wrong at once — does the mechanism fail twice as badly, or is one
wrong part already enough that a second one changes nothing?"**

Write a response in three or four sentences. It may defend your original claim,
revise it, or say precisely what you would need to check to answer the attack.
Preserve your original explanation unedited; write the response as a separate,
new piece of text.

<details>
<summary>Compare after responding</summary>

A response that only restates the original explanation more firmly has not met
the Challenge — it has not engaged with the specific objection. A response that
reaches the rung explains, using the mechanism itself, why the two-fault case
resolves the way it does (for the lock: the plug is one object, so a second
wrong stack duplicates a verdict the first already reached, rather than adding
to it). If your mechanism is different from the lock, the same test applies:
does your response use a real feature of the mechanism to answer the objection,
or does it just repeat confidence?

</details>

## 3. If you are working with a partner

Swap your section 1 placements before comparing them with the lecture's worked
responses. Find one performance you placed differently from your partner and
argue it out loud for two minutes each way, without either of you consulting
the worked response first. Then read the worked response together and note
whether it resolved your disagreement or simply gave you both a third opinion
to react to.

For section 2, your partner supplies the attack on your explanation instead of
the one given above — a real objection is often sharper than a scripted one,
and answering an unscripted attack is closer to what Challenge is meant to
measure than answering a supplied one is.

## 4. Name the hidden assumption — 4 minutes

Recall (rung 2) is defined as producing material "without the source in front
of me." Reconstruction (rung 3) is defined as rebuilding relationships "without
the source in view." The absence of a resource is written into both
definitions, not incidental to them.

In your own words, in two sentences, state the assumption this implies about
performances produced *with* a resource available, and name one performance
from section 1 where that assumption changed how you scored it.

## 5. Keep a short record

| Field | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Placements | Your rung for each of A–E, and which one you would now argue differently. |
| Disagreement | The specific placement you or your partner contested, and the strongest objection either side raised. |
| Challenge response | Whether it engaged the objection using the mechanism, or restated the original claim. |
| The assumption | Your two-sentence statement from section 4. |
| Carried forward | One question this record raises that you expect next week to answer. |

Working alone, you cannot independently verify whether your own Challenge
response is doing real work or merely sounds like it is — that is the same
limit Week 3 named for a self-administered reconstruction. Record the condition
("no partner; self-assessed") rather than treating the record as more certain
than it is.
