## Where Week 10 left us

Week 10 showed why a performance with a collaborator cannot automatically be attributed to one person. This week turns that limit into an examination decision and retrieves Week 9’s prior-state record. We reuse Week 5’s seven rungs: Recognition, Recall, Reconstruction, Explanation, Justification, Challenge and Boundary. They classify responses under stated conditions; they do not certify understanding.

## Purpose and preparation

This self-guided activity applies [Week 11's lesson](/lectures/week-11/).
Allow about 20 minutes to design your instrument and roughly another 20 to 30
minutes to administer it and record the result — you will need to arrange
that second block of time with another person, so read this whole page before
you start.

**This week's core task requires a real second person.** A solo rehearsal is
offered below as a fallback, but read section 5 before choosing it: it states
plainly what it loses, and that loss is not small. You are examining another
person's understanding, not completing a technical certification or a graded
test of them — choose a topic they will not find punitive to discuss, and ask
them first.

## 1. Choose a topic and a person — 5 minutes

Pick someone you can realistically talk with for 20 to 30 minutes: a
housemate, a friend, a family member, a classmate. Pick a topic they
genuinely have some standing to know — a hobby, a skill from work, something
they have actually practised — not a topic from this course, and not one
chosen because you suspect a weak spot.

Ask them directly: "Can I talk to you for about 20 minutes about [topic]? I'm
doing a course exercise about how to find out what someone actually
understands, not just what they can say." Getting a plain yes matters; this
is not a task to spring on someone unannounced.

## 2. Make your five decisions — 5 minutes

Write down, before the conversation happens:

1. **Topic and person** — who, and what, and why they have standing to know it.
2. **Resources** — will they have notes, a phone, or a reference available
   during the conversation, or not? State the one-sentence reason, tied to
   what you actually want to find out.
3. **Notice** — what will you tell them in advance? Full notice of every
   question, partial notice of the topic only, or none? State the reason.
4. **Target rungs** — write three or four tasks (questions or short prompts),
   each one labelled with the rung from
   [Week 5's ladder](/lectures/week-05/) it is aimed at: Recognition, Recall,
   Reconstruction, Explanation, Justification, Challenge, or Boundary.
5. **Live collaborators** — whether another person or AI may contribute during the examination, and why.

Keep this written record; you will compare it with what actually happens.

### Collaborator decision and prior-state record

**Decision 5: live collaborators.** State whether another person or AI may contribute while the subject answers, and why. Wren excludes live collaborators to examine Petra’s own responses; an assisted version would require a record of who contributed what and a claim about the joint performance.

**Before the first task**, ask the subject to record one claim about the topic, a reason and their confidence, before consulting anything or receiving feedback. Keep it unchanged beside the final answer. This Week 9 prior state establishes what was recorded at that point, not everything they previously knew.

For example, a fictional prior note might say: “A finer grind needs less extraction time because water contacts more surface; confidence: medium.” Later agreement shows that the claim was recorded before the tasks; it does not prove that every later justification was already available.

## 3. Administer it — 15 to 20 minutes

Have the conversation. Ask your prepared tasks roughly in order, but let a
genuine follow-up happen if an answer raises one — Week 11's worked example
found real Boundary-rung evidence from an unplanned follow-up, not only from
the four prepared tasks. Do not read from a script rigidly if it makes the
conversation stiff; the tasks are a plan, not a transcript to recite.

Write down what your subject actually says, as close to their own words as
you can manage, during or immediately after the conversation. Do not tidy or
improve their answers afterward — a record you have edited toward your own
expectation is no longer evidence of what they said.

## 4. Classify and interpret — 5 to 10 minutes

For each answer, write down which rung it reached and the specific words that
earned it. Some may land on a rung you did not aim a task at; record that
honestly rather than forcing it onto your original plan.

Then write, in two or three sentences, what this record does **not**
establish — a specific limit, not a general hedge. "They might be wrong" is
not specific enough; "this does not establish whether their explanation
existed before I asked, only that they could produce it when asked" is.

## 5. Solo route, and what it loses

If you genuinely cannot arrange a second person, you may design an
instrument and answer your own questions as a rehearsal, keeping the same
five-decision structure. Do this only as a fallback, and record explicitly
that it is one.

**State plainly what this loses:** you already know every answer before you
ask the question, so nothing about your own performance can be unanticipated
in the way Petra's Boundary answer was unanticipated by Wren. You will
practise the design skill from sections 2 and 3 of Week 11's lesson, but you
will not produce genuine examination evidence, because an examination
requires an independent second mind producing something the examiner could
not have written themselves. Record this limitation in your own words rather
than skipping past it.

## 6. Keep a short record

Keep, in a document or on paper:

| Field | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Prior state and collaborators | The original claim, reason and confidence; the collaborator policy and any contributions actually made. |
| Decisions | Your topic, subject, resources decision, notice decision, and target rungs, as written before the conversation. |
| Transcript notes | What your subject actually said, close to their words, for each task and any real follow-up. |
| Rungs reached | Which rung each answer reached, with the specific evidence. |
| Surprise | Any answer that reached a rung you had not aimed a task at. |
| Remaining uncertainty | What this record specifically does not establish. |
| Route taken | Real administration, or solo rehearsal — and if solo, what section 5 says was lost. |

This record is what Week 12 asks you to bring back and interpret against a
harder question: what are you actually justified in concluding from it?
