# Policies and support

## AI use

You may use AI for anything in this course — reading, drafting, checking your
own reasoning, even generating the fabricated argument in Week 12. That
permission isn't a loophole; it's the point. This course is not about whether
you used a model, it's about whether you, or anyone, can tell the difference
between a claim you understand and one you're repeating well. Every
assessment is designed on that assumption: they test what a transcript alone
can't settle, not whether you produced the words yourself.

What isn't permitted is presenting someone else's understanding — human or
model — as a test result about your own. If an instrument you administer
returns a fluent answer, your job is to say what that fluency does and
doesn't tell you, not to accept it as proof.

## Sources and the "close the tab" ritual

Several weeks ask you to consult a source, close it, and then reconstruct or
apply what you read without reopening it. This isn't a memory trick, it's the
week's instrument: what survives the closed tab is what the exercise is
actually measuring. Keep a plain record of what you read and when you closed
it — the record is part of what you submit, not an honesty formality.

## Late work and extensions

Extensions are granted routinely when requested before a deadline, through
the standard university extension process — there's no separate process for
this course. Work submitted late without an approved extension is marked as
submitted, with the lateness noted; there is no penalty schedule to look up,
because the aim is to know what you can do, not to model a workplace
deadline.

## Getting help

Idris runs the first review of each week's submission; Marisol chairs the
weekly session and sets each week's instrument. Ask during a session where
possible — a live answer is itself a small instrument for whether the
question was the right one to ask.
